The golden rule of AI assistants

Share as little personal information as possible, whichever tool you choose. Leave out names, addresses, account numbers and details that could identify you or someone else. Privacy protections reduce risk; they do not make every question anonymous.

For privacy-first general questions: Duck.ai

Duck.ai is my starting recommendation because it is easy to try and has useful privacy protections. Ask for an explanation, a draft using made-up details, or help with an everyday task.

  1. 1

    Open Duck.ai

    Visit duck.ai. Free use requires no account and has daily limits. Choose from the available models; there is no need to learn all their names.

  2. 2

    Keep the question general

    Try “Explain how a heat pump works” or “Draft a polite thank-you note.” Do not paste identifiable medical, legal or financial records. AI answers are not a substitute for professional advice.

  3. 3

    Check your chat-saving settings

    Recent chats are saved on your device unless you turn that off. Delete chats you do not need, especially on a shared device. Optional Sync & Backup saves encrypted history on servers instead.

For finding and explaining information: Perplexity

Perplexity helps find and explain information; Manus helps turn instructions into finished projects. Their abilities overlap, but that is the simple distinction behind these recommendations.

  1. 1

    Ask a non-personal research question

    Use Perplexity to compare garden tools, research a destination, or learn about a news event. Keep your personal circumstances out of the question.

  2. 2

    Turn off training if you have an account

    Go to Settings → Preferences → Artificial Intelligence and turn AI data retention off. This opts out of future training collection, not all storage or processing.

  3. 3

    Open the sources

    Check that the linked source actually supports the answer and is current. A citation is a starting point, not proof that an AI answer is correct.

For building websites and other projects: Manus

Manus is an AI agent: it can plan a task, use tools, work with uploaded files, and produce a result over several steps. You describe what you want rather than writing every line of code.

  1. 1

    Start with a small, non-sensitive project

    Try Manus for a simple club website, a page layout, or a spreadsheet made with sample data. Explain the result you want and ask for a draft first.

  2. 2

    Limit files and account access

    Upload only what the task needs. Do not upload secrets, passwords or private records. Avoid connecting broad email, storage or other accounts unnecessarily; review permissions and remove access afterward.

  3. 3

    Review before publishing or taking action

    Check sources, wording and files, and test websites or code. Ask an experienced person to review anything that handles logins, payments or personal data. Set clear limits: ask for approval before publishing, deploying, sending messages or spending money.

!
Keep sensitive records out of these tools

Do not enter Social Security numbers, bank or card numbers, passwords, confidential documents, or identifiable medical, legal or financial records. Use made-up examples, and remove personal details from files as well as questions.

Cloud privacy is not complete anonymity

When you use cloud assistants such as Anthropic Claude, ChatGPT, Google Gemini or Microsoft Copilot, your request is processed on remote computers. Products differ in what they collect, save, share and use for training. It is not accurate to say that every AI company trains on everything you type.

A no-training promise is not the same as no storage, no human access or no legal disclosure. Turning off training is also different from deleting your account or hiding your identity. Check the policy for the product and plan you actually use.

What Duck.ai protects, and what it does not

DuckDuckGo sends requests on your behalf, removing your IP address and other identifying metadata before they reach the model provider. Its agreements prohibit using Duck.ai prompts and answers to train models. That makes it a useful privacy-first choice.

The privacy terms allow provider retention of up to 30 days, with limited safety and legal exceptions. The current help page describes stronger zero-retention arrangements for available models, with specific exceptions such as temporary memory caching. Do not read either as “nothing is ever processed or saved anywhere.”

Recent chats are stored locally unless disabled. Optional synced or shared chats are stored encrypted; anyone with a share link can read that chat. DuckDuckGo also collects anonymous usage statistics and broad topic categories with general details such as platform and approximate country. If you choose to share a conversation as feedback, it may be stored and reviewed.

Most importantly, hiding an IP address cannot erase identifying details inside your question or an uploaded file. Leave them out, even with a privacy-first tool.

Another privacy-first option: Proton Lumo

Duck.ai is not the only credible choice. Lumo by Proton offers no conversation logs and zero-access encrypted saved history, meaning Proton cannot decrypt that stored history. Its AI still processes your message on Proton-controlled servers to answer it.

Lumo does not train on ordinary chats. Optional feedback can share an anonymized conversation and, for Apertus, support model training with your consent. Optional web search sends a simplified request to search partners. Account, payment and operational data have separate policy provisions. It is a privacy-focused alternative, not a promise of total anonymity.

Perplexity: useful research, with data trade-offs

Perplexity searches for information and links to sources, but answers can still be wrong. Its consumer privacy notice covers prompts, uploaded content, account details, IP and device data, usage information and advertising measurement. That is why I recommend it for non-personal research, not private records.

For Free, Pro and Max accounts, its help says AI data retention is on by default. Turning it off opts out of future training collection. It does not remove previously collected training data or stop processing needed for service operation, legal compliance and product improvement. Account deletion is a separate process with retention exceptions. Enterprise protections differ.

Use the training control, review optional cookie and advertising choices, and keep questions impersonal. None of these settings makes you anonymous.

Manus: useful development, with wider permissions

Manus can create websites, generate and edit code, and deliver files such as documents and spreadsheets. Because it acts across several steps, a task can involve more than your initial question: uploads, tool results and connected-account content may also be processed.

Its privacy policy covers prompts, files, generated content, task logs and device data, and sharing with service providers and third-party AI providers. Retention depends on the data, purpose and settings, with security and legal exceptions. It is a cloud development service, not a privacy-first chat tool.

Manus publishes no-training protections for its Team offering. Its general policy also permits aggregated or de-identified data to improve models and related technology. Check your plan’s current protections and controls; do not assume a no-training promise means nothing is stored.

Grant only the access needed for the task. Disconnecting an account stops future access, but does not by itself delete content already saved in tasks, files or memory. Review outputs and permissions before anything goes live or costs money.
