Easy Win

Social Media

These platforms are designed to keep you scrolling and harvest your data. You don’t have to quit everything, but you can downsize.

My recommendation

Take Back Control

You don't have to delete everything at once. Start by locking down your settings, then decide what you actually want to keep.

The honest truth about social media

I’m not going to tell you to delete everything. Your friends and family are on these platforms, and that matters. But every platform you step back from, or lock down, is a genuine win for your privacy. Start with the worst offenders.

Facebook: Leave if you can

Facebook collects information about your activity, including information businesses send it from other websites and apps. Privacy settings can limit what other people see and how some information is used, but they do not make Facebook private.

My recommendation: Delete your account if you can.

If you’re staying on Facebook, start here

Find your settings: On a computer, click your profile picture → Settings & privacy → Settings. On iPhone or Android, tap Menu (the three-line button) → Settings & privacy → Settings.

Below, Accounts Center may be called Meta Account. Select Facebook when asked. Menus vary; each help link has device-specific directions. Public help checked November 25, 2026.

  1. 1

    Confirm a recovery email

    Accounts Center → Personal details → Contact info. Check for an email you can open. If needed, add one and enter Facebook’s emailed confirmation code. Keep your phone number for now. Email help.

    Check your result: Your reachable email is listed and confirmed.

  2. 2

    Turn on two-factor authentication

    Accounts Center → Password and security → Two-factor authentication. Choose an authenticator app or security key and finish the confirmation. See our 2FA guide or Facebook help. Then open Additional methods → Recovery codes; print or write them down and store them privately, away from your phone.

    Check your result: 2FA is enabled and your recovery codes are saved.

  3. 3

    Hide displayed email and phone details

    Open Privacy Checkup → Who can see what you share. Set displayed email and phone details to Only me. This hides them from people, not Meta. Visibility help.

    Check your result: Both audiences show Only me.

  4. 4

    Set future posts to Friends

    Continue in Privacy Checkup and choose Friends for future posts. Older posts, Stories, Reels, and public groups need separate attention. Check each post’s audience. Post audience help.

    Check your result: Reopen the future-post setting; it shows Friends.

Optional next steps

  1. 5

    Reduce email and phone profile suggestions

    Settings → Audience and visibility → How people find and contact you. Set People with your email address and People with your phone number to No one. Other suggestions and name searches remain possible. Suggestions help.

    Check your result: Reopen both controls; each shows No one.

  2. 6

    Limit outside-activity personalization

    Accounts Center → Your information and permissions → Activity from other businesses. Choose not to allow this activity to personalize ads and other content, then confirm. Meta can still receive data; this does not stop tracking. Options vary by region/account. Current control help.

    Check your result: The saved choice disallows personalization for your intended account.

  3. 7

    Consider phone removal only if recovery is ready

    Keep your number if you use SMS login codes. Removal is optional, only after a confirmed email, working non-SMS 2FA, and saved recovery codes. If ready, use Accounts Center → Personal details → Contact info → your number → Delete number. Phone help.

    Check your result: The number is gone; your recovery email and non-SMS 2FA remain enabled.

Instagram: The better Meta option

Instagram is also owned by Meta, so it is not a private alternative to Facebook. If you keep it, limit who sees your posts and be mindful of what you share.

If you’re staying on Instagram, start here
  1. 1

    Make your account private

    Open your profile → Menu → Who can see your content → Account privacy. Turn on Private account and confirm. Business profiles must switch account type first. Privacy help.

    Check your result: Private account is on.

  2. 2

    Review your followers and public profile

    Remove followers you do not want seeing your posts; going private does not remove them. Your name, username, photo, and bio stay public. What stays public.

    Check your result: Your followers and public profile match what you want to share.

  3. 3

    Limit outside-activity personalization

    From settings, open Accounts Center / Meta Account → Your information and permissions → Activity from other businesses. Choose not to allow personalization using this activity. Options vary; this does not stop data collection. Current control help.

    Check your result: The saved choice covers your Instagram account.

TikTok: Easy to leave

If TikTok is not important to you, consider leaving or using it less. Reels offers a similar short-video format, but creators and videos differ. Changing apps is not automatically a privacy improvement, and you do not have to replace an app you leave.

Ready to leave TikTok? Start here
  1. 1

    Save anything you want to keep

    Profile → Menu → Settings and privacy → Account → Download your data. Request the file; preparation may take a few days. Download it when ready, before deleting. Data download help.

    Check your result: Your saved file opens.

  2. 2

    Request account deletion

    Profile → Menu → Settings and privacy → Account → Deactivate or delete account. Choose deletion, not temporary deactivation, and complete the prompts. Deletion help.

    Check your result: You completed the deletion request.

  3. 3

    Keep the confirmation

    Save any confirmation shown and follow TikTok’s on-screen notice. Do not log back in just to test whether deletion happened.

    Check your result: You have the confirmation without signing back in.

X (formerly Twitter): Time to go

My recommendation is to leave X if it no longer feels useful to you. If you stay, avoid sharing sensitive information; public posts are not a private space.

Ready to leave X? Start here
  1. 1

    Prepare before deactivating

    Save anything you need. Cancel subscriptions bought through Apple or Google Play separately; deactivation does not cancel them. Revoke access for connected apps you no longer need.

    Check your result: Needed content is saved and any app-store subscription is canceled.

  2. 2

    Deactivate your account

    Settings and privacy → Your account → Deactivate your account. Read the notice and complete the password and confirmation steps. Deactivation help.

    Check your result: You completed the deactivation confirmation.

  3. 3

    Allow 30 days without logging in

    Logging in during those 30 days can reactivate the account. Do not sign in just to check. Uninstalling the app alone does not delete your account.

    Check your result: The full 30 days pass without account access.

💡
You don't have to do this all at once

Pick one platform to leave or lock down this week. Come back and do another one next month. Small steps add up.

How social media platforms make money from you

Every major social media platform is an advertising business. The product they sell is your attention and your data. They collect everything: what you post, what you like, what you scroll past, how long you pause on a video, your location, your contacts, and your behavior on other websites and apps. This data is used to build detailed advertising profiles and sold to thousands of advertisers.

The business model creates a fundamental conflict of interest: the more time you spend on the platform, the more data they collect and the more ads they can show you. This is why these apps are designed to be as addictive as possible.

The Cambridge Analytica moment

In 2018, it emerged that a political consulting firm called Cambridge Analytica had harvested the personal data of up to 87 million Facebook users without their consent, using it to build psychological profiles for political advertising. Facebook had allowed third-party apps to collect not just the data of users who installed them, but the data of all their friends, without those friends’ knowledge or consent.

This was not an isolated incident. It was a window into how Facebook’s data-sharing model worked at scale. The platform has since tightened some of these policies, but the fundamental architecture, a business built on harvesting and monetizing personal data, has not changed.

The attention economy and your mental health

The privacy costs of social media are inseparable from the mental health costs. These platforms are engineered by teams of behavioral scientists to maximize engagement, which means maximizing the emotional responses (outrage, envy, anxiety) that keep you scrolling. Reducing your use isn’t just good for your privacy; it’s good for your wellbeing.
