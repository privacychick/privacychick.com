Social Media

You don’t have to quit everything. Protect your login, choose who can see what you share, and keep the apps that matter to you.

My recommendation

Take Back Control

Start with the four Facebook steps below. Then decide what you want to share less of, use less often, or leave behind.

A manageable place to start

Your friends and family may be on these platforms, and that matters. You can make useful changes without leaving today. Take one step at a time, and check that each change has saved.

Facebook: protect your login, then limit what you share

Protecting your login and choosing who sees your posts are different jobs. The first two steps help you keep access to your account. The next two limit what other people can see. Neither makes Facebook a private service.

Checked November 25, 2026: These directions use official public help pages, not a live account test. Menu names can vary by device, region, and account rollout.

First, choose your device

Open the instructions for the device you are using. Keep this guide open while you work in Facebook.

Computer: using Facebook in a web browser

On Facebook, click your profile picture, then Settings & privacy. Choose Settings for account settings.

For the guided privacy review, choose Privacy Checkup from Settings & privacy. See official Computer Help for Privacy Checkup.

iPhone: using the Facebook app

Open Facebook and find the Menu button, sometimes shown as a three-line icon. Tap Settings & privacy → Settings.

For the guided privacy review, tap Privacy Checkup in Settings. See official iPhone App Help for Privacy Checkup.

Android: using the Facebook app

Open Facebook and find the Menu button, sometimes shown as a three-line icon. Tap Settings & privacy → Settings.

For the guided privacy review, tap Privacy Checkup in Settings. See official Android App Help for Privacy Checkup.

For login and contact settings, look for Accounts Center or Meta Account inside Settings. Meta is gradually changing these menus. If asked, select the Facebook account you want to change, not an Instagram account.

My screen looks different

Open the official help link for your device above. On other Facebook help pages, choose Computer Help, iPhone App Help, or Android App Help if a device selector is offered.

If Privacy Checkup is missing, update the Facebook app and switch to your original main Facebook profile. You can also open Privacy Checkup directly or use Accounts Center in your browser. You may need to sign in.

Do not change an unrelated setting just because its name sounds similar. If you are unsure, stop and ask someone you trust to help with the official directions.

  1. 1

    Confirm a working recovery email

    From Settings, open Accounts Center / Meta Account → Personal details → Contact info. Make sure an email address you can actually open is listed for Facebook.

    If you need to add one, choose Add new contact → Add email, select Facebook, and enter the confirmation code Facebook sends to that email. Do not remove your phone number yet. See Facebook’s email confirmation instructions.

    Check your resultYou can open that email inbox, the address is listed for the correct Facebook account, and you have completed Facebook’s email confirmation.

  2. 2

    Add a second login check and save recovery codes

    Two-factor authentication adds another check after your password. Open Accounts Center / Meta Account → Password and security → Two-factor authentication, then select Facebook.

    Choose an authenticator app, which makes login codes on your phone, or a security key, a small physical device, if you can manage one. Follow Facebook’s two-factor setup instructions and finish the requested code or key confirmation. Just opening the setup page does not turn it on.

    Then open Additional methods → Recovery codes. These are backup login codes for when your usual method is unavailable. Print or write down the ten single-use codes and store them somewhere private, away from your phone. Do not share them. If you use text-message (SMS) login codes instead, keep the required phone number.

    Check your resultFacebook shows your chosen two-factor method as enabled, its setup confirmation is complete, and your recovery codes are saved offline where you can find them if your phone is lost.

  3. 3

    Hide displayed email and phone details

    Use your device instructions above to open Privacy Checkup. Choose Who can see what you share. Set the audience beside your displayed email and phone details to Only me.

    This hides those details from other people, not from Meta. On a computer, another route is your profile’s About → Contact and Basic Info, using the audience selector beside each detail. See official help for contact visibility.

    Check your resultThe review or profile editor shows Only me beside each email or phone detail you changed.

  4. 4

    Set future profile posts to Friends

    Continue in Privacy Checkup to the posts section and choose Friends for future posts. The Settings route is Audience and visibility → Posts → Who can see your future posts? → Friends → Done. See Facebook’s future-post audience instructions.

    This does not automatically change older public posts. Stories, Reels, tags, and public groups also need separate attention. Learn More explains those limits. Check the audience before every post because an individual post can have a different audience.

    Check your resultReopen the future-post setting and confirm Friends. You can also start a new post without publishing it and check the audience label below your name.

Do next: optional changes

The four steps above are a good stopping point. Come back to these when you are ready.

  1. 5

    Reduce profile suggestions based on email and phone

    Open Settings → Audience and visibility → How people find and contact you. Open People with your email address and choose No one. Do the same for People with your phone number. On a computer, the opening button may say View.

    These controls are separate from hiding contact details in step 3. Facebook can still suggest your profile using other information, and people can still search for you by name. See official help for email and phone profile suggestions.

    Check your resultReopen both settings and confirm No one for each.

  2. 6

    Limit personalization using activity from other businesses

    Open Settings → Accounts Center / Meta Account → Your information and permissions → Activity from other businesses. Choose the option that does not allow this activity to personalize ads and other content, then finish any confirmation.

    Read which connected accounts or profiles the choice covers. Wording and choices vary by region, especially in Europe and the UK. If it is missing, use Meta’s current control instructions or the Accounts Center browser link above.

    This is not a “tracking off” switch. Businesses can still send Meta information about your activity. The choice limits specified personalization uses, not all collection or use. See what Activity from other businesses controls. Older Off-Facebook Activity directions are not the current starting point; see Learn More if your menu still has the older control.

    Check your resultReopen the setting and confirm the saved choice does not allow this activity to personalize ads and other content. Check the account scope shown on screen.

  3. 7

    Consider phone removal only if recovery is ready

    Removing your phone number is optional. Keep it if you use text-message (SMS) login codes. Only consider removal after you have a confirmed recovery email, an enabled non-SMS two-factor method, and saved offline recovery codes.

    If any of those is missing, keep the number and use the visibility and suggestion controls instead. If you decide to remove it, open Accounts Center / Meta Account → Personal details → Contact info → your number → Delete number and confirm. Follow Facebook’s phone-number instructions.

    Keep your current Facebook session open while checking. Removal does not prove Meta has erased historical information or a number obtained elsewhere.

    Check your resultThe number is no longer listed, while your confirmed recovery email and non-SMS two-factor method remain enabled and your recovery codes are still safely saved.

My recommendation: Leave Facebook if you no longer need it, or reduce what you share if you do. That is a personal recommendation, not a ranking of which platform is worst for privacy. If you choose to leave, use Facebook’s official account-deletion instructions.

Instagram: choose your audience, not a privacy upgrade

Instagram is also owned by Meta. A private account limits who sees your posts, but it does not stop Meta’s collection of information. Moving from Facebook to Instagram is not automatically a privacy improvement.

  1. 1

    Make a personal account private and review followers

    In the Instagram app, open your profile and the Menu button. Under Who can see your content, open Account privacy and turn on Private account. Finish any confirmation. Use Instagram’s official private-account instructions if your menu differs.

    Review your existing followers and remove anyone you do not want seeing your posts. Making the account private does not remove them automatically. Your name, username, profile photo, and bio remain public. Business profiles cannot be private without switching account type. See what stays public on a private account.

    Check your resultAccount privacy shows Private account is on, and you have reviewed the followers who already have access.

  2. 2

    Review Meta’s personalization control

    From Instagram’s settings, open Accounts Center / Meta Account → Your information and permissions → Activity from other businesses. Check that the choice covers your Instagram account. Follow Meta’s current instructions to choose not to allow this activity to personalize ads and other content.

    The available choices vary by account and region. This replaces the old “Data from partners” toggle instructions. It does not stop businesses sending data to Meta or make your account private from Meta.

    Check your resultThe saved setting does not allow that personalization use, and the account scope includes the Instagram account you intended to change.

TikTok: use less if it is not important to you

If TikTok is not important to you, consider leaving or using it less. Keep the apps you genuinely need and share less sensitive information.

Instagram Reels offers a similar short-video format, but creators, communities, and individual videos can differ. Changing apps is not automatically a privacy improvement, and you do not need to replace an app you leave.

X (formerly Twitter): my recommendation is to leave

My recommendation is to leave X if it no longer feels useful to you. You may value people or conversations there, so make the choice that fits your life. This is an editorial recommendation, not a claim that nobody has a good reason to stay.

Before deactivating, review any connected third-party apps and revoke access for apps you no longer need. If you have a paid subscription bought through Apple’s App Store or Google Play, cancel it separately through that store. Deactivation does not cancel those app-store subscriptions.

Open Settings and privacy → Your account → Deactivate your account. Read the notice, choose Deactivate, and complete the password and confirmation steps. See X’s official deactivation instructions for your device.

Do not log in for 30 days if you want permanent deletion. Accessing the account during that period can reactivate it. Uninstalling the app alone does not delete the account.

Check your resultYou completed X’s deactivation confirmation and, if relevant, your app store shows the subscription canceled. Do not log back in just to check: allow the full 30 days without account access.

Login security, audiences, and company collection

A confirmed recovery email, two-factor authentication, and recovery codes help you keep access to your account. They do not decide who can see a post. A Friends audience or private account limits visibility to other people, but it does not stop the platform from collecting information.

Facebook phone numbers can have different uses. A number supplied specifically for two-factor authentication has a security use; numbers added elsewhere, such as your profile or Marketplace, can have other uses, including ads. This is why phone removal is optional and comes after recovery setup. See Facebook’s explanation of two-factor phone numbers.

Older posts and audiences that need a separate check

Choosing Friends for future Facebook posts does not automatically change older public posts. If you want to restrict previous posts shared with Public or Friends of friends, consider Limit Past Posts under Settings → Audience and visibility → Posts. Read Facebook’s Limit Past Posts instructions and warnings before confirming.

This is optional, and there is no one-click undo. To restore broader audiences, you would need to edit posts individually. Custom audiences are not changed, and this setting does not control posts made by someone else.

Check your resultIf you used Limit Past Posts, inspect the audience label on a previously public post. Do not assume every item on your profile has become private.

Stories and Reels have their own audience controls. Contributions to public groups remain public. Tagging someone can expand a post’s audience, and your setting cannot make someone else’s post private. See Facebook’s post-audience rules and exceptions. People who can see a post can also copy it or take a screenshot.

What the activity control does, and does not do

Businesses using Meta’s tools can send information about activity on other websites and apps. Activity from other businesses controls specified uses of that activity to personalize ads and other content, including Feed and AI responses. It does not stop businesses collecting or sending information. Meta says it may still anonymize that activity or use it to improve products.

Activity within Meta, customer-list advertising, and information you deliberately connect to Meta have separate treatment. Read Meta’s explanation of the setting rather than treating it as a switch that stops all tracking.

Meta announced in June 2026 that Your activity off Meta technologies, previously called Off-Facebook Activity, was being retired in several countries as part of changes to activity controls. That is why the Quick Guide starts with Activity from other businesses.

My account still shows the older activity control

If you still have Your activity off Meta technologies, the legacy route is Accounts Center → Your information and permissions → Your activity off Meta technologies → Manage future activity. Follow the official legacy instructions to Disconnect future activity. This control or its help page may no longer be available in your region.

Disconnection separates activity from your account. It does not stop the underlying collection or sending of data, or delete all data.

Check your resultIf you completed the legacy change, Manage future activity shows future activity is disconnected. That is not proof that tracking has stopped.

How many social platforms make money

Many large social networks earn money from advertising. They use information about your activity to choose content and place ads, and may receive information from other businesses. What they collect and how they share it varies by service.

Selling targeted ad placement is not the same as selling everyone’s personal profile to advertisers. Meta says it does not sell personal information. That does not make its collection and targeting harmless. Audience settings limit what other people see, not necessarily what the company collects.

More time in an ad-funded app can mean more opportunities to show you ads and learn from your activity. Sharing less and using an app less can reduce your ongoing participation, but does not erase information already collected.

A historical lesson: Cambridge Analytica

In 2018, reporting revealed how Facebook data obtained through an app reached the political consulting firm Cambridge Analytica. The app could collect information about users and their friends under Facebook’s rules at the time. The Guardian’s original investigation is a historical example of why access and sharing rules matter, not a description of today’s settings or proof of a platform ranking.

Make room for the parts of life you enjoy

If an app leaves you feeling anxious, or keeps taking time you wanted for something else, reducing your use may help. Other people find valuable friendships and support online. Pay attention to your own experience rather than assuming the same choice is right for everyone.

You can start by checking less often, turning off unwanted notifications, or taking a break. You do not have to replace an app you stop using.
